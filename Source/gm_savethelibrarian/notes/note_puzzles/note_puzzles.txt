Puzzle #4: Develop the Structure of Your Paper.

//Nala Vel: It is important to craft the structure of your paper to determine what your
research process will look like. Place the items in order of the typical research
structure. This is how you will order and organize your ideas. (Have a hint that pops
up when they struggle that basically tells them how to order it.

This is the correct order:
1 - Introduction. An introduction basically includes your thesis statement and the "why"
of your paper to inform the reader on what it will be about.
2 - Literature Review. The literature review backs up the claims you made by including
information from previous research.
3 - Methodology. The methodology section is the manner in which you go about
conducting your own research.
4 - Results. The results section is the outcomes of your original research and contains
the statistics and relevant qualitative and quantitative information you have gathered.
5 - Analysis. The analysis goes over your discoveries from your research and connects
it to your broader thesis. It also shares how these findings are important.
6 - Conclusion. Your conclusion is a way to wrap up your paper and summarize the entirety
of the essay and your research in a way that neartly concludes your ideas.
7 - References. This is very important. Always, and I mean always, cite the ideas of
others in the reference section. There are many different styles of references for you
to choose, but be careful that it is in the correct formatting.

Puzzle #7: Citing Your Sources.

//John Ra: It is vital to cite your sources in the last part of your paper titled "References."
There the student will have complete documentation of the of the origins of their sources cited
within the text of the paper. This is important because it proves that the paper has credibility
and avoids the chances of potential plagiarism. You might have heard of MLA, APA, or Chicago as
these are the most popular types of citations. Since we don't know which one you will be using
let's gather some information that might be useful for the development of a citation.

Show a document and have them find the: author, date, title of the work, the journal
name, the volume and issue number, the DOI or website link.

APA format: Graphy, B. L. (2026). Empirical analysis of the saving of librarians through the
power of research. *The Journal of Librarianship 1(1). https://doi.org/....

Now you haVE created your own citation.
